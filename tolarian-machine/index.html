How it works

Three parts, each built so the next can trust it.

  1. The engine

    Two-player Magic with the stack, priority, combat, and London mulligans. The engine alone decides what's legal, every game replays exactly from its seed, and rule invariants are checked after every game.

  2. The agents

    Agents only ever choose from the legal actions the engine offers. A heuristic player sets the bar; a policy-and-value network learns from self-play and imitation, then has to beat a league of past versions to be promoted.

  3. The broadcast

    Matches stream as public events only: life, board, combat, and the stack, but never a hand, a library, or a mulligan choice. A gateway turns that into the show you see on Twitch.

Learning the rules, one card at a time

Magic has tens of thousands of cards. The engine grows in small, fully tested slices.

Each new mechanic is chosen by how many real cards it unlocks, then measured against the 733 official preconstructed decks: how many are fully playable, and how many are a few cards away. The Jiang Yanggu precon was the first 60-card precon to play in full.

Recently added: planeswalkers and attacking them, fight, spells with more than one target, extort, evolve, affinity, lure, protection, hybrid mana, modal spells, infect and poison, undying and persist, and gaining control of permanents.

Up next: proliferate, unearth, mill, divided damage, convoke, replacement effects, and a layer system.

What you'll see on stream

An unattended show, styled like a card-game client rather than a debugger.

  • Full games at a readable pace, with both players' cards and each important decision on screen.
  • Every seat labeled honestly with the agent and deck playing it. When the learning model isn't seated, the stream says so.
  • The workbench between matches: match generation, training, league evaluation, and deck search, with the latest results.
  • Factual commentary generated from the game itself, plus a lo-fi soundtrack.
